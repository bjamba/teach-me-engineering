What Tailscale is fixing

A short recap. Five problems we've described. Five things Tailscale handles for free. Then we go install it.

1Five problems with traditional networking

You've seen each of these in the last five lessons. Stacked together, they're why "just connect to my home server" felt impossible:

  1. Private IPs aren't routable. Your 192.168.x.x address is meaningless outside your home. Anyone trying to reach it from the internet has nothing to go on.
  2. NAT drops incoming traffic. Even if someone knew your public IP, your router blocks unsolicited inbound packets. Port forwarding pokes a permanent hole; nobody enjoys this.
  3. Public IPs change. Your ISP rotates your public IP whenever it feels like it. Static IP costs extra. Dynamic DNS works but is brittle.
  4. DNS doesn't know your devices. The world's DNS knows google.com, not laptop. To make your devices reachable by name you'd have to run your own DNS server — which is its own project.
  5. Firewalls are everywhere. Even if NAT didn't drop the packet, your OS firewall might. Setting these correctly is a tax most people don't want to pay.

Read those five again. Each one is solvable individually. Solving all five at once, on every OS you own, securely, is the unreasonable thing — and that's what Tailscale does.

2What Tailscale gives you

For each problem, the corresponding fix:

  1. Each device gets a stable, private IP in the 100.x.y.z range. These IPs work for every device on your tailnet, regardless of where they are physically.
  2. NAT traversal opens direct paths peer-to-peer using the simultaneous-outbound trick. No port forwarding, no permanent firewall holes.
  3. Public IP changes don't matter. Tailscale handles the discovery and reconnection automatically. Your devices find each other regardless of how their underlying public IPs shift.
  4. MagicDNS gives every device a name. ssh laptop works. http://home-server works. No DNS server to run.
  5. Tailscale's installer configures the OS firewall to allow tailnet traffic. You don't fight the firewall; your tailnet just works through it.

The aha: Tailscale isn't doing anything magical. It's solving five well-understood networking problems and gluing the solutions together with great UX. The "magic" is just that nobody else has bothered to do all five at once.

3What Tailscale isn't

Useful to be clear:

  • Not a privacy VPN. Tailscale isn't NordVPN or Mullvad — it's not designed to hide your traffic from your ISP or fake your country. It connects your devices to each other. (You can use a Tailscale exit node to get some of this — covered in M4.)
  • Not a corporate VPN. It's not a tunnel into a remote office network either. It's a flat, identity-based mesh between devices you own (or your team owns).
  • Not a cloud service running your apps. Tailscale doesn't host anything. Your services run on your own machines. Tailscale just gives them a path.

Tailscale is the plumbing between your devices. What you do with that plumbing — host AI, share files, run a media library — is up to you. The rest of this course is about doing exactly that.

4What's next

You now have the conceptual scaffolding. M2 covers what Tailscale specifically is, technically — the WireGuard underpinnings, the mesh topology, how the company's coordination service relates to your data. Then M3 gets it running on your devices.
