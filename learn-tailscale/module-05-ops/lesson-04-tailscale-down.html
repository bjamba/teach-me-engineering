What if Tailscale goes down

Reasonable concern. Here's the actual blast radius — much smaller than you'd think — and what to do about it.

1Two failure modes

Coordinator outage: Tailscale Inc.'s coordination servers are down. New device joins fail. ACL changes don't propagate. Existing connections keep working — devices already know each other's keys.

Company shutdown / service termination: extreme scenario. Tailscale ceases operating entirely.

The first happens occasionally and resolves in hours. The second has never happened, but worth thinking about.

2Coordinator outage: what works, what doesn't

Continues working:

  • Existing tailnet connections (devices keep their keys)
  • SSH, Serve, Funnel for already-established services
  • MagicDNS (cached on each device)

Stops working:

  • Adding new devices
  • Removing devices (so a stolen-laptop revoke is delayed until the coordinator returns)
  • ACL edits
  • Cert renewal (eventually a problem if outage is days long)
  • NAT traversal handshakes for newly-active connections (already-active connections persist)

For most personal use, an outage is invisible. For production, plan around the management-plane downtime.

3What to do during an outage

  1. Don't panic. Existing connections work.
  2. Check status.tailscale.com to confirm.
  3. Defer device additions and ACL edits until it's resolved.
  4. If you absolutely need to bring up a new device during the outage, you can configure WireGuard manually using existing keys — but this is rare and painful. Better to wait.

4The "what if Tailscale shuts down" scenario

If you're worried about long-term dependence on the company, you have two paths:

  • Periodically export your config. The admin console lets you download your ACL policy. Save backups. If you need to reconstruct your network on a different stack, the ACL is a complete spec of who-reaches-what.
  • Run Headscale (next lesson). Open-source, self-hosted coordinator. Your tailnet keeps working with no Tailscale Inc. dependency.

For most users, free-tier Tailscale + occasional ACL backup is adequate. Power users who want zero dependency: Headscale.

5Operational practices that limit the blast radius

  • Don't put your auth flow for critical home access exclusively behind Tailscale. Keep a backup path (a wired-in keyboard you can plug into your home server, a console cable to your router) for true emergency local access.
  • Use --accept-routes sparingly. If your phone routes everything through home and home goes offline, your phone has no internet. (Toggle off when home is unstable.)
  • Backup your ACL policy. tailscale-policy.hujson in your dotfiles repo is fine.
