How it compares

Tailscale shares a name with several other tools you've probably heard of. They solve different problems. Knowing which is which is half the battle.

1NordVPN, Mullvad, ExpressVPN — "consumer VPN"

These are privacy VPNs. You connect to a server in another country; your traffic appears to come from that country; your ISP can't see what websites you're visiting. The whole point is to hide traffic, often to bypass geo-restrictions.

Different category from Tailscale. Tailscale isn't trying to hide you from your ISP. It connects your devices to each other.

You can use a Tailscale exit node to get one chunk of consumer-VPN benefit (route your phone through home wifi when you're on hotel wifi — covered in M4 + the travel project). But Tailscale is not a substitute for a privacy VPN, and a privacy VPN is not a substitute for Tailscale.

2Corporate VPNs (Cisco AnyConnect, Pulse Secure, OpenVPN at work)

These tunnel you into a company's office network so you can reach internal resources. Hub and spoke — everyone connects to the company's VPN concentrator.

Same use case as Tailscale, very different design. Tailscale replaces these for many companies — mesh topology, identity-based access, less administrative overhead. If you're using Tailscale at work, this is the kind of tool it's replacing.

3Cloudflare Tunnel (and ngrok)

Cloudflare Tunnel exposes a service on your local machine to the public internet by establishing an outbound connection to Cloudflare's edge. Anyone with the URL can hit your service. ngrok works similarly.

Adjacent to Tailscale Funnel (covered in M4) — both expose a local service to the world. Differences:

  • Cloudflare Tunnel and ngrok always route public traffic through their relays. Tailscale Funnel does too — Tailscale's DERP servers act as the entry point.
  • Tailscale's primary use case is private (between your devices). Funnel is the optional public-exposure feature on top. Cloudflare Tunnel and ngrok are public-first.
  • Tailscale's free tier is more generous for personal use. Cloudflare Tunnel's free tier is also great but ties you to a Cloudflare-managed domain.

Pick Tailscale if your default need is private. Pick Cloudflare Tunnel if your default need is public-facing.

4Headscale — self-hosted Tailscale

HeadscaleHeadscale — an open-source, self-hosted reimplementation of Tailscale's coordination server. Same Tailscale clients on your devices, but your own server brokering connections instead of Tailscale Inc.'s. is an open-source rewrite of Tailscale's coordination server. The Tailscale clients still run on your devices unchanged, but they connect to your own Headscale instance instead of Tailscale Inc.'s servers.

This is the answer to "what if I don't want to depend on Tailscale the company at all?" You run your own coordinator on a $5/mo VPS or a Raspberry Pi at home, and your tailnet becomes truly self-hosted.

For most people, Tailscale's free tier is fine. Headscale becomes interesting if you have lots of devices, want full data sovereignty, or are running a self-hosting community. There's a Headscale project in the Gallery.

5NetBird, ZeroTier — direct competitors

NetBird and ZeroTier solve the same problem as Tailscale (mesh networking with NAT traversal) using different protocols. They're real alternatives. Differences are mostly in user experience, ecosystem, and pricing.

You'll find online debates about which is "best" — they're roughly comparable. This course teaches Tailscale because of its identity-based ACL system and its dominance in the self-hosting community, but the concepts transfer.

6The summary table

NordVPN-style       → hide your traffic (different category)
Corporate VPN       → tunnel into one office network (Tailscale replaces this)
Cloudflare Tunnel   → expose to public (Tailscale Funnel is similar)
Headscale           → self-host the Tailscale coordinator
NetBird / ZeroTier  → direct competitors with different trade-offs

Three quick questions

Q1.You want to bypass Netflix's geo-restrictions and watch content from another country. Which tool fits?
Right. Geo-shifting your apparent country is what consumer VPNs do. Tailscale is for connecting your own devices, not for spoofing locations.
Different category. Tailscale connects your own devices.
Cloudflare Tunnel exposes services to the public; doesn't help you watch geo-restricted content.
Headscale is self-hosted Tailscale; same use case as Tailscale.
Q2.You want zero dependence on Tailscale the company — fully self-hosted control. What's the path?
Right. Headscale is an open-source coordination server. The Tailscale clients on your devices work unchanged; they just connect to your server. Project in the Gallery.
Paid tier still uses Tailscale's coordinator. For full self-hosting you need Headscale.
NordVPN is a consumer VPN, totally different category.
Custom DERP relays exist, but the coordination server is the more central piece. Headscale replaces both.
Q3.You want to share a single web service with strangers (a public website). Which is most natural?
Right. Tailscale's basic mode is private (only people on your tailnet). Funnel adds public exposure on top. Cloudflare Tunnel does the same job from a different angle.
Plain Tailscale is private only. You'd need Funnel for public access.
Headscale changes who runs the coordinator, doesn't help with public exposure.
Wrong tool entirely.
