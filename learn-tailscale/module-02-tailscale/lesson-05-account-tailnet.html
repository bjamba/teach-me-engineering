Account, tailnet, devices

The three nouns you need to keep straight before we install anything.

1Account

You log into Tailscale with an existing identity provider — Google, Microsoft, GitHub, Apple, or a custom OIDC provider. There's no separate Tailscale password. Whatever email you use to log in becomes your Tailscale account.

Every device you add to your tailnet is associated with this account (or another account in your tailnet). When ACLs reference "you," they mean the account, not the device.

2Tailnet

Your tailnetTailnet — a single private network in Tailscale. Personal accounts get one tailnet automatically; that tailnet contains all your devices and any users you invite. Companies usually run one tailnet per organization. is the bag holding all your devices and invited users. A free personal account gets one tailnet automatically. Companies usually have one tailnet per organization.

Tailnets have a domain name like your-name.ts.net (Tailscale auto-generates one; you can rename it in the admin console). That's how MagicDNS knows which network "laptop" refers to.

The free tier supports 100 devices and 3 users per tailnet. Plenty for personal use; if you outgrow it, paid plans go up from there.

3Devices

A device is anything running the Tailscale client — laptop, phone, server, container, router. When you "add" a device, you log into Tailscale on it and it joins the tailnet. The act of installing is the act of joining.

Each device gets:

  • A tailnet IP in the 100.x.y.z range (CGNAT space, doesn't collide with home networks)
  • A name (its hostname, normalized) usable via MagicDNS
  • An owner (the user who added it)
  • Optional tags (covered in M4)

You'll see all of this in the admin console at login.tailscale.com/admin. We'll tour it in M3.

4Inviting other people

You can invite up to 2 other users to your free tailnet (3 total). They use their own email login but join your tailnet. Their devices show up alongside yours. ACLs decide what each user can reach.

This is what makes the "tech support for parents" project work — your parent has their own login, but their machine sits in your tailnet so you can SSH in.
