Verifying it works

Five quick checks. If all five pass, your tailnet is real and ready for the projects.

1Two devices, both online

Open the admin console. You should see at least two devices listed, both with green "Connected" indicators.

If a device shows yellow or red, it's not actively connected. Open Tailscale on that device and click "Connect" / make sure it's logged in.

2Status from the CLI

On any device with the CLI (Mac, Linux, WSL, Windows in PowerShell), run:

tailscale status

Output looks like:

100.64.10.5    your-laptop     you@     macOS    -
100.97.2.23    home-server   you@     linux    active; relay "lax", tx 124B rx 88B
100.115.42.1   your-phone  you@     iOS      offline

Each line is one device: tailnet IP, name, owner, OS, connection state.

3Ping by name

From any device, ping another by hostname (this confirms MagicDNS is working):

ping home-server     # Mac/Linux/Windows
# Should resolve to 100.x.y.z and show round-trip times

If the name doesn't resolve, MagicDNS is off. Go to admin console → DNS → enable MagicDNS.

4The Tailscale ping

Tailscale has its own ping that also tells you whether you're connected directly or via a DERP relay:

tailscale ping home-server

The first reply might say "via relay 'lax' in NNNms" — meaning DERP. After a few seconds, it usually upgrades to "direct" once NAT traversal succeeds. Both work. Direct is faster.

Both directions matter. If tailscale ping fails, check that the destination device is online and that no ACLs block the traffic (default is allow-all; we haven't restricted anything yet).

5SSH works

If your home server has an SSH server running:

ssh you@home-server

That should land you at a shell on your home server, regardless of where you're physically connecting from. Your phone, your laptop on hotel wifi, a friend's couch — all the same. This is the moment. The thing you came here to do works.

6Troubleshooting if something's off

  • Device shows offline — open the Tailscale app on that device, log out, log back in. The most common fix.
  • Names don't resolve — MagicDNS is off. Enable in admin console → DNS.
  • Can't reach a service even though ping works — service isn't running, or OS firewall is blocking the port. See M1L5.
  • Everything seems fine but slow — you're probably going through a DERP relay (your network may have symmetric NAT). Functionally fine, just slower.

7You're done with onboarding

Foundations + onboarding complete. Your tailnet exists. Devices are talking. From here, every module is about using the network you just built.

M4 covers the Tailscale toolbox — Serve, Funnel, ACLs, exit nodes, subnet routers. After that, you can pick projects from the gallery.

Three questions

Q1.You ran ping home-server and got "name resolution failed." What's wrong?
Right. "Name resolution failed" specifically means DNS, not connectivity. Enable MagicDNS in the admin console.
This isn't a Tailscale break; it's a configuration miss.
Offline would say "destination unreachable" or similar. "Name resolution failed" is DNS specifically.
Firewall blocking ICMP would say "destination unreachable", not "name resolution failed."
Q2.tailscale ping says "via relay 'lax'." Is that bad?
Right. DERP is the safety net. Often the connection upgrades to direct after a few seconds. Even if it stays on DERP, it's encrypted and works.
Working as designed.
Not a security issue. DERP is Tailscale's relay infrastructure.
Free tier includes DERP.
Q3.You're at a coffee shop, your home server is at home. ssh you@home-server works. What just happened?
Right. This is what M1 was preparing you for. The five problems are gone. You can now host things.
You didn't. Tailscale's NAT traversal opened the path.
Your router didn't change. Tailscale tunneled through.
Direct connection by default, with DERP only as fallback. The data plane is between your devices.
