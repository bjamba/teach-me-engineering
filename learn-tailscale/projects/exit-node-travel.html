Featured project

✈️ Exit node for hotel wifi

Route your phone or laptop's traffic through home when you're on suspect public wifi. The hotel sees only encrypted Tailscale bytes; everything else looks like you're at home.

Featured Travel Intermediate ~30 min Prereqs: M4L5 (Exit nodes)

Why this project

Hotel and airport wifi networks have a long history of being snooped on, hijacked, or just plain misconfigured. Commercial VPNs solve this for a monthly fee. Tailscale exit nodes solve it for free, using infrastructure you already control: you point your phone or laptop at your home router as the egress point, and your local network sees only encrypted traffic.

It's not a privacy VPN — your traffic appears to come from your home, and your ISP sees what they always do. But it's perfect for "I don't trust this wifi for the next week."

Who this helps

  • You travel with a laptop and want a no-thought way to bypass shady wifi
  • You want banking/streaming services to "see" you as being at home
  • You're frustrated by hotel content filters or captive-portal weirdness

What you get

  • An always-on home device offering itself as an exit node
  • One-tap toggle on your phone/laptop to route everything through home
  • Hotel/airport wifi can't see your domains or contents — just encrypted Tailscale
  • Your laptop's apparent location: home. Banks, streaming services, geofences all happy

Prerequisites

  • Tailscale running on an always-on home device (Pi, NUC, server)
  • Tailscale running on your travel laptop and/or phone
  • M4L5 read for context on exit nodes

Steps

Pick an exit-node host at home ~3 min

Any always-on home device with Tailscale works. Pi, NUC, server, NAS. Linux is easiest because of the IP forwarding step.

Enable IP forwarding (Linux) ~3 min

echo 'net.ipv4.ip_forward = 1' | sudo tee /etc/sysctl.d/99-tailscale.conf
echo 'net.ipv6.conf.all.forwarding = 1' | sudo tee -a /etc/sysctl.d/99-tailscale.conf
sudo sysctl -p /etc/sysctl.d/99-tailscale.conf

macOS doesn't need this step.

Advertise this device as an exit node ~2 min

sudo tailscale up --advertise-exit-node

You may need to re-authenticate.

Approve the exit node in admin console ~2 min

Admin console → Machines → click your home device → Edit route settings → toggle "Use as exit node" on. Save.

Until you approve, the device is "advertising" but not actually available.

Use it from your phone ~2 min

  1. Open the Tailscale app on your phone.
  2. Tap Use Exit Node.
  3. Pick your home device.

Verify by visiting whatismyipaddress.com — you should see your home's public IP, not the hotel's.

Use it from your laptop ~2 min

Mac/Windows: Tailscale menu/tray → Exit Node → pick your home device.

Linux: sudo tailscale up --exit-node=home-server. Toggle off with sudo tailscale up --exit-node=.

Toggle off when home ~1 min

You don't want exit-node-through-home enabled when you're physically at home — it just adds latency. Toggle off via the same UI.

Auto-toggle tip: Tailscale doesn't currently auto-toggle by location. Some users wire this up via Shortcuts (iOS) or Tasker (Android) keyed to wifi SSID — when you connect to your home wifi, disable the exit node automatically.

Verify

  • With exit node enabled on your phone, public IP lookup returns your home IP
  • With it disabled, public IP lookup returns the local wifi's IP
  • Bandwidth feels reasonable for browsing (a noticeable hit on big downloads is expected)

Variations

  • Cloud exit node: spin up a $5/mo VPS in any region. Install Tailscale, advertise as exit node. Now you have an exit point in another country — useful for testing geo-locked content.
  • Travel router: see the GL.iNet travel router Gallery project — a portable hardware version that exit-nodes everything connected to it.

Caveats

  • Some sites detect VPN-like traffic; banking apps occasionally complain. Exit-noding through home is usually less suspicious than commercial VPNs because your home IP is residential.
  • Latency: every packet detours through home and back. For chat/email, fine. For online games, no.
  • Bandwidth: bottlenecked by your home upload speed. If you're in Europe and home is in California, expect noticeable latency.
  • This is not a privacy VPN. Your ISP sees the same things they always do.

Where this leads

  • GL.iNet travel router — hardware version that does this for every device on its wifi
  • Pi-hole — pair so your phone gets ad-blocked DNS while exit-noding through home
