📡 GL.iNet travel router

Pocket-sized hardware router that runs Tailscale baked in. Plug into hotel ethernet, get instant home network on every device — without configuring each one.

Gallery Travel Advanced ~1 hr Prereqs: Existing tailnet, ~$70 hardware

Why this project

The exit-node-on-laptop project handles your laptop. But what about your iPad, Kindle, partner's phone, the kid's Switch, the Apple TV you brought? A pocket travel router runs Tailscale once, broadcasts its own wifi, and every device that connects to that wifi inherits the home network. One install, every device covered.

GL.iNet routers (Slate, Beryl) ship with first-class Tailscale support in their firmware. You set it up at home, then anywhere you go, you plug it into hotel ethernet (or repeat hotel wifi), connect your devices, and they think they're at home.

Who this helps

  • You travel with multiple devices and don't want to onboard each one to Tailscale
  • You bring an Apple TV / Chromecast / iPad on trips and want them to "just work" with home services
  • You'd rather solve the captive portal once (on the router) than on every device

What you get

  • A pocket router preconfigured to join your tailnet on power-up
  • Its own wifi network — connect any device, get tailnet access automatically
  • Optional: routes everything through your home as exit node
  • Hotel captive portals only need to be solved on the router, not on every device

Every device that connects to the GL.iNet's wifi inherits tailnet access. Captive portal handled once on the router; everything else is transparent. Optional: route everything through your home as exit node.

Prerequisites

  • An existing tailnet (M3 done)
  • A GL.iNet router with built-in Tailscale support — Beryl AX (MT3000) is the current pick at ~$70–100

Hardware notes

  • Minimum: GL.iNet Slate AX or older Beryl — fine for laptops + phones
  • Comfortable: Beryl AX (MT3000) — current generation, plenty of horsepower for streaming
  • Excellent: Flint AX1800 if you want a more permanent home travel-router setup

Most modern GL.iNet routers ship Tailscale in their firmware.

Steps

Initial GL.iNet setup ~10 min

Power on. Connect to its default wifi. Open http://192.168.8.1 in a browser. Run through the wizard — set admin password, wifi name and password. Make sure firmware is current.

Install / configure Tailscale ~10 min

In the GL.iNet web UI: Applications → Tailscale (or similar wording).

  1. Click Install if not already.
  2. Click Authenticate. Copy the URL it shows.
  3. Open that URL in a browser, log into your tailnet, authorize.
  4. The router shows up in admin console as a tailnet device.
  5. Optional: enable "Use Tailscale as exit node" — now everything connecting to the router routes via your home exit node.

Test at home ~5 min

Connect your laptop to the GL.iNet's wifi. Visit whatismyipaddress.com. If you set "use as exit node," you should see your home's public IP. Otherwise the router's normal upstream IP.

Try visiting http://home-server. If it resolves, your tailnet routing works.

Use it on the road varies

  1. Plug into hotel ethernet (or set up "Repeater" mode to bridge hotel wifi).
  2. Connect your devices to the router's wifi.
  3. Done. Every device you connect now has tailnet access transparently.

Verify

  • Router appears in admin console as a tailnet device
  • Devices connected to the router's wifi can reach home services by hostname
  • Public IP lookup shows home IP (if exit-node mode is on)
  • Repeater mode works with a typical hotel wifi captive portal

Common issues

  • Captive portal won't load — temporarily disable Tailscale on the router, solve the portal, re-enable.
  • Slow speeds — bottlenecked by hotel uplink and your home upload speed. Not a router issue.
  • Some devices can't reach tailnet hostnames — ensure MagicDNS is on in your tailnet and the router's DNS settings forward to Tailscale.

Caveats

  • Hotel ethernet quality varies wildly — your bandwidth bottleneck is the hotel, not the router
  • Some hotels' captive portals require specific browser quirks; the GL.iNet UI helps but can be fiddly
  • Treat the router as a tagged device (tag:travel) and ACL accordingly — it's basically a movable subnet router
