Subnet routers

Reach devices on your home LAN that don't have Tailscale installed. One Tailscale-equipped device acts as a gateway to the rest of your home network.

1The problem

Some devices can't run Tailscale: a smart bulb, a printer, a NAS with weird firmware, your router's web UI, a TV. They live on your home LAN at 192.168.1.X, but Tailscale won't ever route to 192.168.1.X directly — those addresses are private to your home network and won't traverse a tailnet.

A subnet router bridges this. One device on your home LAN that does run Tailscale advertises "I can reach the 192.168.1.0/24 subnet — send those packets through me." Your phone in another country can now reach the smart bulb at home.

2Setting it up

  1. Find your home subnet (usually 192.168.1.0/24 or 192.168.0.0/24; ip route shows it).
  2. On a Linux box, Pi, or any always-on home device with Tailscale: 
    sudo tailscale up --advertise-routes=192.168.1.0/24
  3. Approve in admin console: Machines → device → Edit route settings → toggle the route to "Approved."
  4. On client devices that should use this route: 
    sudo tailscale up --accept-routes
    (or the equivalent toggle in the macOS/Windows/iOS apps.)

From the client, 192.168.1.X addresses now route through your home box. Your printer, your bulbs, your router's admin UI — all reachable.

3Linux note: IP forwarding

Same as exit nodes — Linux subnet routers need IP forwarding enabled at the OS level:

echo 'net.ipv4.ip_forward = 1' | sudo tee -a /etc/sysctl.d/99-tailscale.conf
echo 'net.ipv6.conf.all.forwarding = 1' | sudo tee -a /etc/sysctl.d/99-tailscale.conf
sudo sysctl -p /etc/sysctl.d/99-tailscale.conf

Tailscale's installer warns you if this is missing.

4Subnet router vs exit node

  • Subnet router: routes only the specified private subnet (e.g. 192.168.1.0/24) through the gateway. Your traffic to google.com still goes out your local network.
  • Exit node: routes all internet-bound traffic through the gateway.

One device can be both. A common home setup: a Raspberry Pi at home that's the subnet router (so your phone can reach the printer) AND offers itself as an exit node (so your laptop on hotel wifi can route through home).

5Common uses

  • Reach your printer from anywhere
  • Manage your router's admin UI when away
  • Use your home Pi-hole's DNS from your phone (covered in the Pi-hole project)
  • Bridge a remote office network into your tailnet without installing Tailscale on every workstation
