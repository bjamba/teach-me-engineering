Module 5 · Overview·~5 min read·Coming later

Deep Learning Foundations

Neural networks, backprop, and PyTorch. The moment your math from Module 1 becomes the engine behind everything modern — from image recognition to ChatGPT.

Lessons unlock after Module 4. This page is your preview.

Why this matters

Classical ML handles most tabular problems. But when the input is pixels, audio, text, or anything unstructured, neural networks dominate. You need to actually understand them — not just call model.fit() — if you want to confidently ship modern AI systems.

Fair warning: this module is denser than the ones before it. Take your time. The math from Module 1 pays off here.

What you'll learn

Lesson 1 — From Logistic Regression to a Neural Network

A neural net is just stacked logistic regressions with nonlinearities. Build one from scratch in NumPy, then port to PyTorch.

Lesson 2 — Backpropagation Demystified

The chain rule applied mechanically. Watch gradients flow through a 3-layer network by hand, then understand why autograd works.

Lesson 3 — PyTorch: The Working Framework

Tensors, nn.Module, optimizers, DataLoader, GPU tensors. The idiomatic training loop you'll use for every deep model.

Lesson 4 — Training Dynamics

Learning rates, batch sizes, schedulers, initialization, normalization, dropout, early stopping. Why your loss curve looks weird and how to fix it.

Lesson 5 — Convolutional Networks (CNNs)

Vision in one lesson. Convolutions, pooling, the ResNet trick. Transfer learning with pretrained models.

Lesson 6 — Sequences: RNNs to Attention

Why RNNs/LSTMs struggled, why attention won. The bridge that sets up Module 6 (transformers).

Exercises you'll build

  • A neural network trained on MNIST from scratch in NumPy (no PyTorch) — then the same network in 20 lines of PyTorch.
  • Transfer learning: fine-tune a pretrained ResNet on a small custom image dataset.
  • A sequence model that predicts the next character in a text stream — a direct on-ramp to Module 6.
Hardware note

Most exercises will run fine on CPU or free Google Colab GPUs. We won't push into territory that needs paid hardware until Module 6's fine-tuning experiments, and even there we'll use small enough models to stay free.
